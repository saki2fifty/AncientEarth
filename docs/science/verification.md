# Mathematical verification

Run the independent math crate tests without a renderer:

```powershell
cargo test -p earth-math
```

The active application uses the analytic WGS84 ellipsoid at `h=0` with height-free regional categories. Legacy terrain, imagery and source-data tests remain as historical regression coverage; they do not describe the current physical surface. ## Optional weather-informed optical paths

The 14 groups in [`refraction_review.rs`](../../crates/earth-math/tests/refraction_review.rs) test the physical equations, weather ingestion and finite numerical implementation separately. They do not certify that a coarse forecast reproduces the atmosphere above a real beach. The current model and its declared profile transplantation are described in [refraction.md](refraction.md).

The phase-index fixtures use all six 633nm dry-air rows and three wet-air rows in [NIST Table 1](https://emtoolbox.nist.gov/Wavelength/Documentation.asp). The expected indices are published values, rather than values generated by the implementation. Dry cases allow `5.1×10⁻¹⁰` for the table's nine-decimal rounding; wet cases allow `10⁻⁹`, also accounting for the independently published saturation pressures rounded to whole pascals. These comparisons validate the Ciddor implementation under those conditions, not its extrapolation to every upper-air sample. Numeric-domain, vacuum, dispersion, specific-humidity and unit-label checks are separate.

Independent ray oracles include straight rays in constant index, exact radial integrals of piecewise-linear `n(h)`, and equatorial conservation of `b=n(r)r cos(elevation)`. In the equatorial plane, WGS84 geodetic height is exactly `r−a`. Separate composite-Simpson quadrature of `dφ/dr=b/[r√((nr)²−b²)]`, `ds/dr=nr/√((nr)²−b²)` and `dℓ/dr=n²r/√((nr)²−b²)` supplies expected surface positions and lengths. Three descending-ray fixtures agree within 30mm; this finite-case tolerance is not a bound for all trajectories. A synthetic positive index gradient produces a turning ray that cannot reach the ground by the same conserved invariant. Thin isolated index bumps at three heights are traced in both directions: their exact excess optical path is 0.1m, within a 2mm test tolerance, ensuring that midpoint stages cannot silently skip them. Other cases check actual top exit, a plane immediately before ground, foreground celestial bodies before atmospheric entry, far-space vacuum entry, invalid data and unresolved searches.

## Reference fixtures

The [GeographicLib CartConvert manual](https://geographiclib.sourceforge.io/html/CartConvert.1.html) gives:

```text
latitude 33.3°, longitude 44.4°, ellipsoidal height 6000 m
ECEF = (3816209.60, 3737108.55, 3485109.57) m
```

Expected coordinates are published to centimetres; the test uses half-centimetre comparison tolerance. This is the precision of the printed fixture, not an assertion that the algorithm has centimetre numerical error.

The [IOGP/EPSG Guidance Note 7-2](https://www.iogp.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/373-07-02.pdf), printed page 96, supplies another conversion example at latitude 53°48′33.820″ N, longitude 2°07′46.380″ E and ellipsoidal height 73 m. Its ECEF values are rounded to millimetres.

Additional axis, 45° latitude/longitude, and axis-parallel-ray expectations use algebraic expressions evaluated independently with 70-digit decimal arithmetic. Grazing tests use exact axis-aligned constructions to distinguish numerical uncertainty from an inaccurately rounded tangent angle.

## Numerical scope

The supported geodetic height range is −10 km to +1,000,000 km. Near-surface conversion roundtrips use sub-micrometre tolerances; at the extreme height endpoint the tests allow larger floating-point spacing. Ordinary downward eye-to-ellipsoid ranges are checked at nanometre-scale tolerances in double precision. These are regression thresholds for chosen numerical cases, not a measured accuracy guarantee over all inputs.

Tangent rays require special treatment because the mathematical problem is ill-conditioned. The intersection routine treats discriminant values within `32 × f64::EPSILON × max(1, cross_squared)` of zero as possible tangency. A hit/miss within that numerical band cannot be promised as physically exact.

The renderer's finite pixels and image colors are intentionally outside the precision claim. The measurement ray is calculated directly at the camera's exact center. Unlimited magnification, source resolution and absolute physical accuracy are not claimed.

## Terrain-specific evidence

An independently authored analytic plane fixture computes post heights that put all ECEF vertices in a known plane. Expected ranges therefore come from a plane equation rather than the implementation's triangle routine. Scaled directions, edge/vertex hits and nearly parallel rays exercise this fixture.

For varied rough terrain, 1,200 deterministic rays compare the BVH with brute-force traversal. This verifies acceleration consistency; the separate analytic plane expectations test the intersection mathematics. Review also verifies exact-footprint masking in the canonical scene, a valid far ellipsoid root after a masked near root, below-ellipsoid ground placement, high-speed feet collision and minimum-height interior rejection. ### Angular disks versus spherical integration

[`verify_eclipse_disk_error.py`](../../scripts/verify_eclipse_disk_error.py) independently integrates the actual overlap of two spherical caps. In solar-centered polar coordinates it uses the azimuthal interval selected by the Moon-cap dot-product inequality, with `sin(θ)` as the solid-angle measure. Multiplying this integrand additionally by `cos(θ)` computes uniform-radiance flux projected onto a plane perpendicular to the solar-center direction. These two reference quantities have different normalizations; neither includes limb darkening or an atmosphere.

The 459-case sweep uses solar angular radii 0.0045, 0.00465 and 0.0048 rad, lunar/solar radius ratios from 0.9 through 1.1, and contact/interior separations. The largest sampled difference from the angular-plane area is **`8.775×10⁻⁷` of the solar disk for spherical solid angle** and **`1.280×10⁻⁶` for projected uniform-radiance flux**. Doubling Gaussian quadrature from 256 to 512 nodes changes a result by at most `3.45×10⁻¹⁵` in this sweep. The integrals, measures, endpoint substitution and reported results were independently reviewed. This quantifies the approximation on the sampled Earth-viewing domain; it is not a proof for every geometry, particularly close approaches to the Moon.

## Reproducible frame pacing

Run a native build with `--frame-capture artifacts/timing.json`, separately from screenshot mode. Collection begins only after both 120 application frames and 10 seconds have elapsed since the requested presentation became ready. Preparation that becomes pending before collection resets both thresholds. The interval that crosses the warmup boundary is excluded. The next complete intervals are recorded until their sum reaches at least 20 seconds, retaining the final complete interval. This is intended to exclude initial preparation; any later compilation, upload or scheduling cost remains measured.

The JSON preserves every positive finite `Time<Real>` interval, the actual adapter and window presentation mode, physical dimensions, build profile, camera/projection, surface/grass quality, boundary/UI/panel flags and astronomical clock settings. It also reports view changes, unfocused frames and incomplete-scene frames. If the view changes, focus is lost or presentation becomes incomplete during recording, all intervals remain in the data and the analyzer marks the result ineligible as a stationary-scene baseline. These are application frame intervals, including scheduling and presentation pacing; they are not GPU timestamp queries or a universal hardware benchmark.

Run `python scripts/analyze_frame_capture.py artifacts/timing.json` for the summary. With `n` intervals and total time `T`, aggregate throughput is `n/T`, rather than the mean of reciprocal frame times. Mean and median intervals are reported alongside nearest-rank percentiles: the 95th and 99th percentiles select sorted one-based ranks `ceil(0.95n)` and `ceil(0.99n)`. Three Rust tests cover warmup, readiness resets and complete-interval collection; three independent Python test groups cover closed-form statistics, rank boundaries and malformed-data rejection. The mathematics lead independently reviewed the collection and analysis methods.

Preparation and warmup have a 300-second application-time watchdog: failure to start recording exits with an error. This is an automation bound, not a performance requirement or an exclusion of slow recorded frames.

For opt-in native movement integration, add `--frame-capture-motion` to a `--coast --paused --frame-capture artifacts/motion.json` run. After ready warmup, the probe calls the existing canonical `TerrainScene::fly` API to lift 5 m and request 120 m of forward travel, respecting its 0.25-second input limit. It requires an actual origin rebase and more than 80 m of eye displacement, waits for recovery, changes quality, disables grass after observing the new generation, checks that visible/query counts are cleared, then re-enables grass and waits for two seconds of ready recovery. The probe logs eye/origin coordinates, generations, blade/bound counts and readiness events; it also requires observing pending work.

Motion runs are explicitly labeled `nonstationary-motion-qa` and are never eligible as stationary baselines. They retain all raw intervals, continue beyond 20 seconds when necessary to finish recovery, and exit with an error if the motion sequence exceeds 180 seconds. This validates native state transitions and matched geometry/query counts; it is not a pixelwise GPU comparison or proof that all possible interleavings are covered.
